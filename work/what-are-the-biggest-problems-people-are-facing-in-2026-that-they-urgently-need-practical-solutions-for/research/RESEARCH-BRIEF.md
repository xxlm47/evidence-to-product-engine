# Research Brief

## Topic

What are the biggest problems people are facing in 2026 that they urgently need practical solutions for?

## Target reader

Everyday people affected by rising costs, economic uncertainty, AI disruption, scams, and rapid changes in technology

## Desired outcome

Identify the most important problems affecting their finances, work, safety, and daily life in 2026, evaluate practical solutions using reliable evidence, and take specific actions to improve their situation.

---

## Research questions

1. What are the most significant problems affecting ordinary people in 2026 across finances, housing, employment, healthcare, technology, personal safety, and daily living?

2. Which of these problems have become materially worse, more urgent, or more widespread during 2025–2026?

3. Which problems are people actively searching for information, advice, tools, or solutions to solve?

4. What evidence demonstrates that people are experiencing these problems at meaningful scale?

5. Which problems have the strongest combination of urgency, frequency, financial impact, and willingness to seek or pay for solutions?

6. What solutions are people currently using for each major problem?

7. Where are existing books, guides, websites, apps, services, and other products failing to adequately solve these problems?

8. What specific underserved audiences or use cases exist within the strongest problem areas?

9. Which emerging problems are likely to become substantially more important during the next 1–3 years?

10. Which opportunities could realistically be addressed through a practical nonfiction book, guide, workbook, checklist, or related digital product?

11. Which opportunities can be researched and produced with a $0 startup budget while maintaining high factual accuracy?

12. Which potential opportunities have credible evidence of demand but relatively weak or poorly differentiated existing solutions?

13. What are the major risks, legal considerations, geographic limitations, or rapidly changing facts that could make a potential product unreliable or difficult to maintain?

14. Based on the evidence, what are the top 10 potential product opportunities?

15. Which single opportunity has the strongest overall combination of demand, urgency, audience size, competition gap, monetization potential, evidence quality, and ability to deliver a genuinely useful outcome?

### Problem

- What exact problem does the reader have?
- How frequently does it occur?
- What makes the problem difficult?
- What happens if the reader does nothing?

### Demand

- Are people actively searching for solutions?
- What language do they use?
- What questions repeatedly appear?
- What evidence exists outside book marketplaces?

### Competition

- What books already solve this problem?
- What products, videos, courses, websites, or communities compete for attention?
- What do competing books promise?
- What do readers praise?
- What do readers complain about?

### Opportunity

- What is missing?
- Who is underserved?
- Can the topic be made more specific?
- Can the promise be made more useful?
- What would make the book meaningfully different?

### Commercial viability

- Who would pay for this information?
- Why would they buy a book instead of using free information?
- What additional value does a structured book provide?
- What adjacent products could logically follow?

---

## Evidence requirements

Each major claim should eventually have:

- Source
- URL or reference
- Date checked
- Claim supported
- Confidence level

Avoid treating AI-generated claims as evidence.

---

## Initial hypothesis

> The strongest version of this project will solve a specific problem for a clearly defined reader rather than attempting to appeal to everyone.
